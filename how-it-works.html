How it works

Compare a page before and after a PeopleSoft update, and see exactly what changed on screen. The inspector compares a screenshot taken before a change with one taken after it, and outlines what moved, appeared or disappeared. It runs entirely in your browser.

What it does

You give it a Before screenshot (the page before an upgrade, patch or customization; also called the baseline) and an After screenshot. It highlights unintended visual changes so a tester does not have to spot them by eye.

It is a supplemental quality-assurance tool for the visual-comparison part of testing, not a replacement for functional or security testing. A visual diff can show that a field or button appeared or disappeared for the tested role. It does not prove that authorization is correct.

The steps

  1. Press Try the demo (30 seconds), or choose Compare my own screenshots and add your own Before screenshot.
  2. Add the After screenshot (drop it, paste it with Ctrl+V, or choose a file).
  3. Press Compare.
  4. Decide each change: Expected, Regression or Noise.
  5. Press Save result and fill in the evidence form.
  6. Sign off, then (optionally) use the After screenshot as the new Before screenshot.

What happens when you press Compare

  1. Line up. If the After screenshot is a few pixels lower, higher or zoomed slightly, the tool lines it up first and tells you how ("The After screenshot was 3 pixels lower; the inspector lined them up automatically").
  2. Difference. It subtracts the two pictures pixel by pixel, after a light blur that ignores tiny rendering noise.
  3. Ignore zones. Areas you marked (such as a timestamp) are set to "no difference".
  4. Threshold. Small differences are dropped; larger ones are kept.
  5. Changes. Nearby changed pixels are merged into boxes, and tiny boxes are dropped.
  6. Similarity and heat map. It also computes a similarity score (SSIM, 0 to 1) and a heat map of where the picture changed most.

The result is FAIL when any change is found or the changed share of the page is above the page's limit (0.1% by default). Otherwise it is PASS. The similarity score is shown for information only; it does not decide PASS or FAIL.

The technology in plain words

OpenCV.js
OpenCV is a well-known library for working with images. OpenCV.js is the version that runs in a web browser. We use it to align, subtract, and find the changed areas. It is included with the app, so nothing is downloaded from another site.
WebAssembly
A compact, fast format that browsers can run next to JavaScript. OpenCV.js is compiled to WebAssembly, which is why comparing two full-page screenshots takes a fraction of a second.
Web Workers
A worker is a second thread in the browser. The heavy image work happens there, so the page stays responsive and the Cancel button always works. If a comparison takes longer than 30 seconds, the worker is restarted.
IndexedDB
A database built into the browser. It keeps your pages, baselines, ignore zones and saved runs on this device, so they are still there after a refresh. You can inspect it in Chrome DevTools under Application, IndexedDB, erpinspector.

Capture the before and after pages with the same browser, 1366×900 viewport, 100% zoom, test user and data, then promote the after capture only after sign-off.

Reading a result

Four views show the same result: Outlined changes (numbered outlines on the After screenshot), Side by side, Slide to compare (0 is all before, 100 is all after) and Heat map (blue means no change, red means strong change). The cards below the views carry the same information as text, with a Before and After picture of each change. Click a card to highlight its numbered box, or click a box to jump to its card.

Change
One area of the page that looks different between the before and after screenshots.
Page changed
How much of the whole page looks different. "0.8% of the page" means a few small spots; 30% or more usually means a theme or layout change.
Similarity
A score from 0 to 1 for how alike the two screenshots look overall. A supporting number only.
Alignment
The tool first lines the screenshots up, so a page captured a few pixels lower is not reported as changed.
Expected
This change was supposed to happen. It is part of the patch or upgrade.
Regression
This change is a defect. Log it. The page can't be signed off until it is fixed.
Noise
Not a real change, for example a time stamp or rendering. Safe to ignore. Not sure? Choose Regression and add a note.

Every change of a FAIL result needs a decision before you can save it. You cannot sign off while any change is marked Regression: log a defect, attach the report, and compare again after the fix. Use the After screenshot as the new Before screenshot only after sign-off. The "What this probably is" text on a card is a guess from the shape, never a verdict.

Ignore zones

An ignore zone hides a rectangle (a timestamp, a user name) from the comparison. Zones belong to the page and are reused on every later comparison. If zones cover more than 15% of the page, the tool warns you that large ignored areas can hide real defects.

You can add one three ways: drag on the Before screenshot, choose Add ignore zone on a change card, or use Add zone manually and type the X, Y, width and height (the keyboard route).

Ignore zones hide areas from the comparison. They are not a security control.

Your data and storage

Everything is stored in this browser, in an IndexedDB database called erpinspector. There are no network calls after the page loads, and your screenshots never leave the device.

To wipe it, open Settings and choose Reset app data. If the app says storage is blocked, allow site data for this site in your browser settings, or leave the private window. You can still try an example and compare, but nothing is kept when you close the tab.

Limits

  • It is a visual check only. It does not test behaviour, calculations or permissions.
  • It needs full-page PNG screenshots captured with the same settings.
  • The demo uses fictional data only ("Demo ERP (fictional)"). Do not load real production screenshots.
  • The time-saving figures in the business case (about 3 minutes by hand versus about 1 minute) are assumptions for a pilot, not measurements.

Try it with real PeopleSoft screenshots

Oracle publishes PeopleSoft's user guides (PeopleBooks) on the public web. Many topics include screenshots of real pages. You can load those images into the inspector in your own browser, so you see how it behaves on a genuine PeopleSoft page, not only on our fictional demo.

  1. Open one of the PeopleBooks links in the list below, in a new tab.
  2. Scroll to the screenshot of the page (for example "Timesheet page" or "Work Location page").
  3. Right-click the screenshot and choose Save image as.... Save it as a PNG, and keep the name <module>_<page>_before_<YYYYMMDD>.png so you can find it later.
  4. In the inspector choose Compare my own screenshots (it reads Add a new page once you have pages) and pick that image as the Before screenshot.
  5. On the Compare screen, add a second image as the After screenshot, then press Compare. Good After screenshots:
    • your own capture of the same page from a test system you are allowed to use (see the capture tip below);
    • the same screenshot after you change it in an image editor (move a button, delete a field, change a label). This is the easiest way to see the inspector flag a change;
    • the same topic from a different PeopleBooks release, if the screenshot really differs (see the note below).
  6. Read the outlined changes. Treat the result as a supplemental QA aid, not as functional or security testing.

Capture tip. The Before and After screenshots must be the same size and zoom: same browser, 1366 x 900 viewport, 100% zoom, PNG, never JPEG (the capture standard above). PeopleBooks screenshots come in many different sizes, so two PeopleBooks images of different sizes are not a fair pair. The line-up step only forgives small scroll and zoom differences.

Why this site does not ship these images. The screenshots are Oracle's copyrighted material, so we do not copy, host or bundle them. This project uses fictional data only and carries no Oracle branding. When you save an image yourself, it goes to your own disk, and the inspector keeps it only in your browser (IndexedDB) on your device. Nothing is uploaded. "Reset app data" removes it. Check Oracle's terms of use before you share or publish any image you saved.

Who built this

This app was designed, built, reviewed and tested by a team of 17 AI assistants, each given a name and a job, such as business analyst, designer, developer or tester. They worked like a small project team: one of them (Atlas, the coordinator) planned the work and handed tasks to the others, and the others checked each other's work before anything was finished. The team is not part of the app you are using. Nothing here talks to an AI while you work: the comparison runs entirely in your browser, and no data leaves your device.
